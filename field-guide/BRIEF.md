# Fable 5.2: A Field Guide — a 40-second low-poly nature documentary

You are directing and building a finished short film for an artist who posts generative JavaScript art and animation on X. It is the artist's showcase post and must be extremely high quality: it should look like a premium animated short / mobile-game cinematic trailer, not a tech demo. Read this whole brief first; follow it, and use your own judgement wherever a detail would look or read better done differently.

## The idea
A nature documentary about a strange new creature: **Claude Fable 5.2**, played by the coral block hero of the artist's "Fable Saga" fight series, rebuilt in faceted low-poly 3D. Big documentary-style captions observe it "in the wild". Each beat is a REAL behaviour from Anthropic's official prompting guide for Fable 5.2, played as a fast 3–4 s comedy bit, then a "HANDLER'S TIP" card shows the fix and the creature's behaviour changes on screen. Developers learn something real (bookmarks, quote-posts); everyone else gets a funny, charming creature.

Three non-negotiables from the artist:
1. **ONE CONTINUOUS SHOT.** No cuts, ever. The camera travels through one connected world from the first frame to the last. Beats hand over to each other through camera moves: flying through a thought cloud into the next place, following an object, whip-pans that land, push-ins through a hole, a crane up over a ridge. It must feel like a single oner / theme-park ride.
2. **SURREAL VISUALS.** The world is a dreamlike low-poly place where ideas are physical: the island floats in a sky of slow-drifting giant faceted letters and thought clouds; the user's messages arrive as enormous objects from the sky; scale is playful (a creature-sized bridge over a canyon that is a crack in a giant keyboard key; sticky notes falling like snow; a report scroll that unrolls into a river). Beautiful, not random: every surreal element means something in the joke.
3. **EASILY UNDERSTANDABLE.** Someone scrolling with the sound OFF must get every beat from picture + one short caption. Gags read in silhouette. Captions are big, few words, on screen long enough to read twice.

## Look
- Low-poly done like a premium game cinematic: flat-shaded facets with a soft warm key light and long soft shadows (shadow mapping, PCF), ambient occlusion feel (baked or SSAO-lite), a gentle sky-gradient fill, atmospheric perspective/fog for depth, subtle depth of field on far objects, a light bloom on emissive things. No harsh aliasing: supersample (e.g. 2×) and downscale.
- Palette: warm cream/sand island, sage and teal grass facets, dusk-lilac sky with soft peach horizon, coral for the creature and anything "alive", lilac/violet for user messages and UI objects, a little gold for tokens/money. Darks rich, never grey; no colour shifts.
- The world is surreal but clean: restraint over clutter. Each beat has ONE readable focal action; background elements are calm.
- Typography: captions rendered crisp by the compositor (not inside the 3D scene), high contrast with a soft shadow/plate, safe from the 4:5 edges; the same type system throughout; the TIP card is a consistent little design element (cream card, glove icon, slides in with overshoot).

## Process: quality bar
Build it roughly end to end first (every beat blocked, the camera path continuous, captions in), then polish in rounds. Each round: render a contact sheet, strips across every hand-over, stills of every beat; LOOK at them with the Read tool including 100% crops; critique as a ruthless animation director, comedy editor and art director. Score 1–10: (a) does it read as ONE continuous shot, (b) every gag understandable with sound off, (c) comedy timing, (d) the creature's acting and charm and fidelity to the saga hero, (e) surreal world design and beauty, (f) lighting/materials/render quality at 100%, (g) caption readability and design, (h) first-second hook, (i) the ending/poster, (j) sound sync and mix. Fix everything below 9 and repeat; expect at least 5 rounds. Watch especially for: a hand-over that feels like a cut; a caption on screen too briefly; a gag that only works if you already know the guide; clutter; the creature going off-model; washed-out darks; aliasing/shimmer on thin geometry; anything flashing (≤ 3 large-area luminance flashes per second; run a flash check). You can't watch at speed: judge motion from strips, and check timings numerically.
