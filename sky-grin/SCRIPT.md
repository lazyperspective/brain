# GUST

*A kid laughs so hard it becomes wind, and the wind takes them on the ride of their life across a living sky.*

- **Length:** 60 s.
- **Format:** vertical, 1080×1920, 24 fps, drawn on twos.
- **Dialogue:** none.
- **Look:** hand-inked lines that boil, flat cel colour with one shadow tone, paper grain, smear frames on fast moves.
- **Rule of the world: you can see the wind.** Every laugh leaves the kid's mouth as a hand-inked ribbon of wind, cream with a gold edge, that curls, spirals and pushes things. The audience learns this rule in the first 10 seconds, and the whole film escalates it.

---

## 0:00–0:03 · HOOK
- **Frame 0:** a gust slams in from the right. The red scarf whips across the whole screen, so it is solid red for 4 frames.
- It wipes away to reveal THE KID on the hilltop, mid-giggle, eyes squeezed shut.
- Grass bows and petals streak past. The camera is low, and the sky is huge.

## 0:03–0:12 · ACT 1: THE LAUGH
1. **Close-up.** A dandelion seed drifts onto the kid's nose. Their cheeks puff up and they try to hold the laugh in. Their whole head inflates like a balloon, with squash and wobbling lines.
2. **"HA!"** The laugh bursts out as a visible ink ribbon of wind. It tears across the hill and flattens the grass in a travelling wave. Flowers pop off their stems and spin into the air.
3. **Wide.** The ribbon hits a fat cumulus cloud and punches a perfect hole clean through it, like a donut.
4. **Beat.** The kid freezes. They slowly look at the hole, then look at us, and a grin spreads ear to ear.
5. They laugh on purpose. The second gust twists a cloud into a spiral. The third is so big it catches the scarf: the scarf unspools into a 20-metre red kite tail, snaps taut and **yanks the kid off their feet.**

## 0:12–0:35 · ACT 2: THE RIDE
1. **Lift-off, worm's-eye view.** The kid rockets up past the camera. **Scale reveal:** the camera pulls back and the hill shrinks to a tiny green island floating in an endless ocean of clouds.
2. **Cloud bouncing.** The kid lands on a cloud top, which squashes like a trampoline, then bounces from cloud to cloud. Every landing leaves a puff and a ring of ink speed lines.
3. **The sky is alive.** A cloud lobe the kid just bounced on **opens one huge eye.**
4. **THE CLOUD WHALE.** A whale made of cloud rises out of the cloud sea. It is enormous; the kid is a speck on its head. It breaches in slow motion, trailing water-vapour waterfalls lit gold at the edges.
5. The kid runs along the whale's spine, laughing. Each laugh paints wind ribbons in loop-the-loops.
6. **The blow.** The whale spouts a geyser of tiny clouds that turn into a **flock of cloud birds**. The cloud wisps from earlier become **schools of cloud fish** swirling around the kid in a tornado of shapes.
7. **Choreography.** The kid conducts them with laughs. Every ribbon the kid laughs out, the flock follows in perfect formation, drawing giant loops across the sky.
   - **Peak frame:** the kid at the centre of a spiral galaxy made of birds, fish and wind.

## 0:35–0:48 · ACT 3: THE STORM
1. **Colour drains.** The blue slides to indigo. A towering storm cloud rolls in, and **the linework changes**: it becomes thick and scratchy, with dry-brush ink and hatching. The storm has a scowling face carved into its folds.
2. **Lightning** is drawn as jagged ink strokes that **crack the paper itself**: white fractures run across the frame.
3. The storm inhales. The wind reverses and rips the scarf away. The scarf vanishes into the dark.
4. **The fall.** A straight-down dive with speed lines, smear frames and rain as slashing ink dashes. The palette is nearly black and white, with only the kid's skin keeping colour.
5. **Silence.** Everything stops mid-fall. Rain hangs in the air, and the kid floats upside down.
6. **The reveal.** For the first time in the film, **the kid opens their eyes.** They are huge and bright, with sky-blue reflections. The kid looks up at the storm face.
7. And they laugh. At the storm.

## 0:48–0:55 · ACT 4: THE BIGGEST LAUGH
1. The laugh becomes a **colossal golden wind spiral** that fills the screen. Ink lines burst outward, and the paper grain glows.
2. The spiral reaches the storm and **tickles it.** The storm's scowl cracks, it snorts, and it **bursts out laughing**: its own giant ink gust.
3. **The storm explodes into sunshine.** Clouds fling open like curtains, petals and confetti rain down, and a rainbow is painted across the sky in one brushstroke.
4. The cloud birds fly out of the light carrying the red scarf. The kid catches it. The scarf opens like a parachute, and the kid sways down through golden-hour light.

## 0:55–1:00 · LANDING (LOOP)
1. The kid drifts down past the camera onto the same hilltop, in the same pose and from the same low angle as frame 0. The grass springs back up.
2. The kid giggles with their eyes squeezed shut. A dandelion seed drifts toward their nose.
3. **The gust slams in and the scarf whips across the screen: CUT TO FRAME 0. The film loops.**

## Sound (no voice)
- **Laugh motif:** a fluttering ocarina run, which gets bigger with every laugh.
- **Act 1:** plucked strings and whistles, with a gust whoosh and a pop for every laugh.
- **Act 2:** a bouncy drum groove. The whale has a low, warm hum. The birds and fish give a flurry of chimes.
- **Act 3:** the music drops out. There is thunder that sounds like paper tearing, and the fall builds into a rising roar.
- **The silence:** a full second of nothing at all.
- **The laugh:** the ocarina motif returns solo, then the whole orchestra lands on the storm's burst.
- **Landing:** the melody resolves, then the gust that starts the loop again.
